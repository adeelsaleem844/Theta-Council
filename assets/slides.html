01
The problem

Most “AI trading agents” hand a model an order endpoint and a prompt that says be careful.

Models are excellent at reading context across a dozen instruments at once.

Models are unreliable about position size on the fortieth API call of the session.

So don't ask them to be.

02
The inversion
The LLM has a veto and a dial. It never has the wheel.

It can

  • reject any trade, with a reason
  • lower conviction → lower size
  • set a posture that only tightens risk

It cannot

  • invent a strike
  • raise a quantity
  • widen a risk limit
  • reach an order endpoint

size_multiplier is clamped to ≤ 1.0. There is no way to ask for more size.

03
The edge · variance risk premium

Selling an option is selling a forecast of movement.

The market prices that forecast at implied volatility. The underlying then delivers realised volatility. On average the first is larger — a fee paid to whoever carries the gamma risk, not a prediction about direction.

vrp_ratio = ATM implied ÷ 20-day realised

  ≥ 1.12   the market overpays for movement   → SELL premium
  ≤ 0.98   movement is cheap                  → BUY  premium
 between   → NO TRADE

The third branch is where a real desk spends most of its life — and the branch nobody demos.

04
Pricing the edge

In dollars, not adjectives.

V_iv  at market implied vol   → what we get paid
V_rv  at 20-day realised vol  → what we think it is worth

spread we SELL:  EV per share = credit − V_rv
spread we BUY :  EV per share = V_rv − debit

edge_ratio = EV ÷ max_loss    ← the ranking statistic

Both legs, same measure, same horizon — so the difference is the variance premium in dollars. The credit is already haircut for the bid/ask we expect to cross, so the edge is quoted after friction.

EV not positive → the structure is never built.

06
What the model is actually for

Arithmetic can't see these three things.

  • Three of today's candidates are the same index bet in three costumes.
  • A 1.4× premium across every megacap at once is a macro print being priced, not five independent gifts.
  • The book has quietly become one-way short puts.

Guardrails. Invented candidate ids are dropped and logged. Silence is not consent — a candidate the model doesn't mention is discarded. On failure or unparseable output the desk continues deterministically and journals that it did.

07
One cycle
  1. IntakeOne brief, shared by every agent, journalled verbatim.
  2. ReadAnalyst forms the regime view; Vol Scout the verdict.
  3. Manage — before entriesClosing a winner returns the risk budget a new entry then uses.
  4. Portfolio gatesKill switches, delta band, buying power, session clock.
  5. DesignReal strikes from the live chain; EV priced after friction.
  6. AdjudicateThe model may veto and may shrink. Nothing else.
  7. ReviewRisk Officer gates each survivor and sets the size.
  8. ExecuteSingle mleg orders — then journal everything, refusals included.

A desk that opens before it closes drifts to its position cap and sits there.

09
Exits get all the money
TIMEThe last quarter of the trade's own life, capped at 7 days. A 32-day spread exits at 7 DTE; a 5-day spread at 1. A fixed rule would close a short-dated trade the moment it opened.
TARGET55% of max profit. The last 45% takes most of the remaining time to earn and carries all of the remaining gamma.
STOP2× the credit received, before max loss.
DEFENDShort-strike delta through 0.40 — this is no longer the trade that was approved.

Refusals are decisions too. Blocked candidates are journalled with the gate that stopped each one and its numbers. A desk that only logs its fills is grading its own homework.

10
Alpaca infrastructure · one intent, three pipes
Alpaca CLI
preferred

JSON on stdout. Subcommands probed with --help, not assumed. The exact argv is journalled — replay any decision by hand.

Alpaca MCP
72 tools discovered

stdio JSON-RPC. Reads tools/list and every tool's JSON schema, then binds intents to what the server actually offers. Late binding, not baked.

REST
guaranteed floor

stdlib urllib. So the desk never stalls because a binary is missing on someone else's machine.

  • Spreads go over the CLI, not just REST. alpaca order submit --order-class mleg --legs '[…]' — and --symbol / --side must be omitted for mleg, because they're per-leg.
  • Credits are negative. The convention has shifted between releases, so the first credit order is sent with the documented sign and, on a price rejection, retried once flipped — then remembered and logged.

Every call records which pipe served it. “We used MCP and the CLI” is a table in the journal, not a claim in a README.
